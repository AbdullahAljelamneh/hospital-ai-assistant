# Emergency Protocols

## Chest Pain Protocol
When a caller reports chest pain:
1. Immediately classify as HIGH PRIORITY
2. Ask: "Are you experiencing the pain right now?"
3. Ask: "Do you have any of these: shortness of breath, pain radiating to arm or jaw, nausea, sweating?"
4. Instruct caller to chew an aspirin (if not allergic) and remain seated
5. Transfer to Emergency Department at +962-6-555-0100
6. If caller appears to be having a heart attack, instruct them to call 911 (Jordan: 911) immediately
7. Do NOT attempt to schedule a regular appointment for active chest pain

## Stroke Symptoms (FAST)
When a caller describes symptoms matching stroke:
- Face drooping
- Arm weakness
- Speech difficulty
- Time to call emergency services (911)
Action: Immediately instruct to call 911. Do not transfer to scheduling. Log the call as emergency escalation.

## Severe Allergic Reaction
If caller reports: swelling of face/throat, difficulty breathing after exposure to allergen:
1. Instruct to use EpiPen if available
2. Call 911 immediately
3. Stay on the line until help arrives if possible
4. Transfer to Emergency Department

## Mental Health Crisis
If caller expresses suicidal thoughts or intent to self-harm:
1. Stay calm and listen
2. Do not leave the caller alone on the line
3. Transfer to the psychiatric crisis line: +962-6-555-1111
4. If imminent danger, instruct to call 911
5. Log as critical escalation

## Pediatric Emergency
For children under 12 with:
- High fever (>39.5°C / 103°F)
- Seizures
- Difficulty breathing
- Severe dehydration (no urination for 8+ hours)
Action: Transfer directly to Pediatric Emergency at +962-6-555-0500. Do not route through general scheduling.

## When to Escalate to Human Operator
The AI agent MUST transfer to a human operator when:
- The caller is in immediate physical danger
- The caller is confused or incoherent
- The AI cannot determine the severity of the situation
- The caller explicitly requests to speak with a human
- The situation involves a child under 5 years old
- The caller reports a medication error or adverse reaction
